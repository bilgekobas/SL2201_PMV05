# Equal PMV ≠ Equal Physiology  

Indoor environments can meet the same **Predicted Mean Vote (PMV)** while differing in **air temperature, mean radiant temperature, air speed, and clothing**. These component mixes may recruit different thermoeffectors that PMV does not resolve.  

In a repeated-measures pilot *(N = 11; 5 male, 6 female)*, we created four office-like scenarios tuned to **PMV ≈ 0.5** and ran **morning** and **afternoon** sessions on separate days.  

We recorded:  
- Wrist skin temperature  
- Tonic electrodermal activity (EDA)  
- PPG/BVP (calculated HR, extracted RMSSD, LF/HF)  
- Thermal sensation (TSV)  
- Comfort (TCV)  
- Alertness (KSS)  

We found:  
- Equal-PMV scenarios produced distinct physiological profiles  
- Scenario effects were largest for **EDA**, then HR and skin temperature  
- Sex differences were modality-specific (greater EDA reactivity in males; wider skin-temperature spreads in females)  
- Time of day modestly elevated HR/EDA  

Illustrative patterns included:  
- Lower male skin-temperature rise with high EDA in the *warm-with-fan / low-clothing* scenario  
- Opposite TSV biases under *cool-air / warm-radiation*  

> “Equal comfort” need not imply equal physiological load.  
This motivates **component-aware design** and **larger confirmatory studies**.
